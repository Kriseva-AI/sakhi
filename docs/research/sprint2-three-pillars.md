# Sakhi: accessibility, usability and UI/UX as product pillars

Primary-source research, accessed **2026-10-19**. This memo records published tools, patterns and governance. It does not claim knowledge of unpublished production stacks, prove that borrowing a component makes Sakhi accessible, or approve a public release. No shared code was edited and no application tests were run for this research.

**Recommended approach:** keep Sakhi's current stack and adopt the leaders' method: reusable semantic components, explicit interaction contracts, documented evidence, review ownership and checks on every change. The three pillars need different evidence. A visual audit or automated score cannot substitute for users completing a sensitive journey successfully.

These are public design-system tools and published practices. They are not proof that every service at those organisations uses that exact combination. Sakhi does not need to install four systems, adopt their branding or reproduce every tool. One tooling caveat: Carbon's [public repository accessibility guide](https://github.com/carbon-design-system/carbon/blob/main/docs/guides/accessibility.md) describes DAP with IBM authentication steps and internal links. Do not present that workflow as an unrestricted public dependency. The current public status page instead names IBM Equal Access. Verify availability, license and maintenance before adding a tool.

## Apply the three pillars to Sakhi

The examples below are recommendations, not claims that the current build passes them.

| Pillar | Specific Sakhi pattern | Evidence to require |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accessibility: can people perceive, operate and understand it?** | Native links for destinations, buttons for actions, labelled inputs, meaningful headings/landmarks, visible focus and a skip link. Keep the direct emergency option reachable before data retrieval. | Keyboard traversal and focus recovery, screen-reader announcements, contrast and forced-colour checks, physical zoom/reflow, text-spacing and mobile assistive-technology tasks. Record combinations actually tested. |
| **Usability: can people achieve the intended task?** | Goal-led entry, minimal questions, skip/back/change choices, plain service comparisons and recoverable errors. A person can learn options without choosing a legal label or retelling an incident. | Moderated fictional tasks measuring finding the appropriate option, recovering from an error, explaining the destination and distinguishing a link visit from a referral or submission. Record observed confusion, not just completion time. |
| **UI/UX: does the experience guide attention and remain coherent?** | Stable navigation, readable hierarchy, consistent control appearance, restrained status labels and a clear next action. Layer optional details without hiding information needed for safety or choice. | Review across narrow screens, long Indic text and RTL; inspect loading, error, empty, stale, offline, expanded and cancelled states. Validate information hierarchy with users, including low-literacy and access-needs participants. |

The separation is a Sakhi operating model, inferred from the published practices. The pillars overlap and should be reviewed together for each complete journey.

## Five immediate interaction decisions

1. **Use ordinary service navigation.** GOV.UK's [navigation pattern](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/navigate-a-service/) says to simplify the journey first and reserve navigation for important top-level sections. Sakhi's broad exploration can have a small set of stable destinations; an individual guided path should not become a sitemap. Its [service-navigation component](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/service-navigation/) also calls for rechecking custom content after dependency updates.

2. **Use disclosure semantics carefully.** W3C's [disclosure navigation example](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/examples/disclosure-navigation/) explains why typical site navigation does not need ARIA menu roles. It documents buttons, expanded state, normal tab order and closing/focus behavior. Its code is illustrative, with possible browser/assistive-technology gaps, especially on mobile. Prefer semantic HTML and test Sakhi's implementation rather than copying the example as production-certified code.

3. **Make errors actionable.** NHS [testing guidance](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/accessibility/testing) requires a clear error near its input and links from an error summary to the affected field. For Sakhi, preserve nonsensitive selections, describe how to recover and keep alternate public help available after retrieval failure. Validation must never demand an intimate narrative. The dedicated NHS error-summary page timed out during this research; the testing page directly supports this pattern.

4. **Make comparisons semantic.** USWDS [table guidance](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/table/) specifies row/column headers and captions, with testing in the actual implementation. Sakhi should compare service purpose, jurisdiction, disclosure and next action through a real table or a properly structured mobile equivalent. Shrinking a visual grid must not remove relationships for a screen reader. Do not make whole-card click behavior compete with separate actions.

5. **Hide secondary detail only.** NHS [details guidance](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/design-system/components/details) uses native `details`/`summary` and warns against hiding information most people need. A centre's longer postal address can be progressively disclosed when its district and action remain clear. Immediate assistance, important eligibility limits, privacy implications and stale-source warnings should remain visible. That content prioritization is a Sakhi safeguarding judgment requiring expert review.

## Maintain quality as the product grows

These are proposed Sakhi practices derived from the sources above:

- **One pattern record per reusable interaction.** Give it an owner, purpose, non-use cases, source rationale, privacy/safeguarding constraints, states, keyboard contract, locale behavior and evidence links. Treat changes to copy and focus behavior as part of the same component.
- **Publish evidence at state level.** Adapt Carbon's matrix: automated, keyboard, manual assistive technology and user research are separate columns. Use precise labels such as tested on a named build, partial, not tested and review pending. A passing default card cannot stand in for its expanded, stale or error state.
- **Separate experiments from approved guidance.** Use a scoped trial lifecycle and a review decision. GOV.UK's published lifecycle includes time-based progression when no negative feedback arrives; Sakhi should not inherit that rule for critical contacts or safety content. Its existing named human approval requirement takes precedence.
- **Gate meaningful regressions in CI.** Reuse local synthetic fixtures for behavior, accessibility scans and visual comparisons at the states most likely to fail. Carbon's published tool choices demonstrate categories of checks; they do not require Sakhi to buy Percy or add a second test framework.
- **Keep performance budgets reviewable.** USWDS [performance guidance](https://designsystem.digital.gov/performance/how/) names Lighthouse and alternatives, distinguishes budgets from longer-term goals and describes build failures when budgets are exceeded. For Sakhi, establish declared local-device/network baselines and asset/timing budgets for critical journeys. Lab checks remain different from physical-device performance. Do not copy historical example thresholds or add user tracking to T0.
- **Control upgrades and contribution intake.** Pin dependency versions, review release notes, verify critical interactions after upgrades and record changed assumptions. Public contributions need a reproducible nonsensitive example and an owner. Keep personal cases out of issues, recordings and screenshots. USWDS's open proposal model is useful governance inspiration, not permission to accept every feature request.
- **Give content its own maintenance loop.** Link source wording, checked date, named review and locale status to the component that displays it. Design-system reuse cannot fix stale support data or unreviewed translation. Keep contact approval and interface approval distinct.

## Human gates that remain

The [NHS research guidance](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/accessibility/user-research) involves people with access needs throughout development, uses working HTML for meaningful assistive-technology research and emphasizes recruitment and session accommodations. Sakhi should apply that inclusion principle through a safeguarding-led, synthetic research protocol. A small convenience sample cannot establish safety or multilingual comprehension.

Before public claims or release, retain these named review tasks:

| Gate | Required scope |
| --- | --- |
| Accessibility specialist and participants with access needs | VoiceOver/Safari, TalkBack/Chrome and an appropriate Windows screen-reader/browser combination; keyboard, speech/switch access where feasible; physical 200%/400% zoom, orientation and low-end Android. Report actual coverage and gaps. |
| Indian safeguarding, legal and appropriate medical reviewers | Crisis visibility, minor boundaries, error/fallback wording, service distinctions and disclosure implications. NHS clinical governance supplies a review principle, not Indian procedures or legal authority. |
| Native-language and domain reviewers | Complete journey comprehension, Indic shaping, mixed-script screen-reader behavior, RTL, long strings and explicit fallback. Key coverage is not reviewed translation. |
| Usability researcher with safeguarding expertise | Consented, fictional tasks; breaks and withdrawal; no forced disclosures; test whether users understand that Sakhi has not sent, booked or dispatched anything. |
| Security/privacy review and physical operation checks | No unintended data persistence or transmission, safe external previews, verified session clearing limits and honest offline behavior. A design-system component does not confer a security certification. |

**Release evidence should state what changed, what was measured, who reviewed it and what remains unknown.** Borrow the maintenance discipline and interaction principles. Keep Indian services, Sakhi's privacy model and local human approval as the authority for safety behavior.

All linked evidence is from the named official design-system/manual maintainers or W3C. Some guidance contains older examples; current version numbers and corporate tool availability must be rechecked before dependency adoption. No outside emergency procedure, institutional endorsement or production-readiness claim is imported.
